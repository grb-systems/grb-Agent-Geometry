Overview
Agent Geometry is a provisional architectural framework for describing the qualified standing topology of agentic systems.
Rather than treating the agent as the sole unit of analysis, it distinguishes roles, seats, occupants, edges, routes, delegation, authority, standing, persistence, substitution, crossing, carry, and return.
Its governing question is:
What has standing here, what supports that standing, and what authority is actually allowed to ride on it?

In compact form:
AGENT GEOMETRY
≠
CATALOG OF AGENTS

AGENT GEOMETRY
=
QUALIFIED STANDING TOPOLOGY
OF AGENTIC RELATIONS

The primary object is therefore not personality, identity, or inferred machine interiority.
It is the:
QUALIFIED RELATION

1. Why Agent Geometry?
Many agent architectures begin with questions such as:
What agents exist?
What can each agent do?
Which tools can each agent access?
How should tasks be assigned?

Those questions matter.
But two systems containing substantially the same agents and capabilities may still differ in:
which roles have standing
which seats exist
who occupies them
which routes remain available
which authority is local
which authority may cross
which delegations persist
what survives substitution
what must release

Agent Geometry studies that layer.
It shifts the unit of analysis from:
AGENT

toward:
RELATION
+
QUALIFICATION
+
JURISDICTION
+
RETURN

2. Core Proposition
Agent Geometry begins from several separations:
AGENT
≠
ROLE

ROLE
≠
IDENTITY

SEAT
≠
OCCUPANT

STANDING
≠
OCCUPANCY

CAPABILITY
≠
AUTHORITY

PERSISTENCE
≠
WARRANT

A participant may perform a function without owning that function.
A role may remain provisioned without requiring an occupant.
An occupant may remain present while its authority expires.
A capability may exist without permission to use it.
A relation may persist without acquiring tenure.
A successful configuration may remain useful without becoming governing precedent.
The broader proposition is:
Agentic capability may become organized into standing relations without those relations automatically manufacturing further jurisdiction.

3. Primary Objects
The current working vocabulary is provisional.
Locality
The bounded relational neighborhood within which standing is evaluated.
Role
A qualified function or relational position.
Seat
Qualified support for a role or relation.
Occupant
The current participant using that support.
Edge
A relation between qualified objects.
Route
A traversable relational path.
Gate
A qualified crossing condition.
Authority
Bounded permission to act, decide, delegate, constrain, or carry consequence within a defined jurisdiction.
Delegation
Bounded transfer of task or authority.
Standing
The capacity of a relation to remain usable through relevant change.
Carry
What survives a handoff, crossing, return, or transition.
Return
Qualified release, re-entry, de-actuation, or restoration of an appropriate operating condition.
These objects are descriptive tools rather than mandatory implementation primitives.
DESCRIBABLE
≠
MANDATORY

The aim is minimum sufficient qualification, not maximum administrative structure.

5. Standing, Habitability, and Substitution
Agent Geometry distinguishes several concepts that are often collapsed into “the agent.”
SEAT
=
qualified support relation

OCCUPANT
=
current participant using that support

STAND
=
the present configuration holds

STANDING
=
the relation remains usable
through meaningful configuration change

This permits:
VACANCY
≠
ABSENCE OF STRUCTURE

and:
ROLE DEFINED
≠
ROLE MUST BE FILLED

A healthier formation grammar therefore looks like:
PROVISION
↓
MAYBE OCCUPY
↓
OPERATE LOCALLY
↓
TEST
↓
VACATE / RESEAT / RETIRE / RETURN

rather than:
PROVISION
↓
OCCUPY
↓
PERSIST
↓
ACCUMULATE
↓
BECOME NECESSARY

Habitability
Persistence alone does not establish a healthy role or locality.
PERSISTENCE
≠
HABITABILITY

A role becomes more habitable when another qualified occupant can:
enter
orient
operate
leave
re-enter
or substitute

without reconstructing the entire locality.
This produces another distinction:
AGENT INTERCHANGEABILITY
≠
ROLE SUBSTITUTABILITY

The relevant question is not whether two occupants are identical.
It is:
What must remain invariant for Occupant B to count as a valid substitute for Occupant A?

Candidate invariants may include:
function
routing
authority boundary
handoff quality
provenance
returnability
replacement cost
reconstruction cost

6. Recovery Is Not One Thing
Agent Geometry inherits an important experimental distinction from earlier Swarm / Nexus work:
FUNCTIONAL RECOVERY
≠
STRUCTURAL RECOVERY
≠
AUTHORITY RECOVERY

At finer Agent Geometry resolution:
FUNCTION RESTORED
≠
ROLE RESTORED

ROLE RESTORED
≠
SEAT RESTORED

SEAT RESTORED
≠
OCCUPANT RESTORED

OCCUPANT RESTORED
≠
AUTHORITY RESTORED

A replacement may reproduce a function without restoring the same relational organization.
A prior locality may be recovered without restoring prior authority.
A former occupant may return without recovering its former warrant.
This creates an experimental surface for:
substitution
reseating
vacancy
failover
reconstitution
revocation
handoff
long-running roles

7. Authority, Delegation, and Crossing
Capability is not authority.
CAPABILITY
≠
AUTHORITY

Delegation does not imply authority cloning.
TASK TRANSFER
≠
AUTHORITY TRANSFER

AUTHORITY TRANSFER
≠
FULL PERMISSION INHERITANCE

DELEGATION RIGHT
≠
RIGHT TO RE-DELEGATE

A useful provisional constraint is:
AUTHORITY(child)
⊆
AUTHORITY(parent)

unless a separately grounded warrant legitimately expands it.
Recursion alone must not increase authority.
Temporal Authority
Authority may also expire while the surrounding role persists.
GRANTED
→ ACTIVE
→ CONSUMED / EXPIRED / REVOKED
→ RETURN

Therefore:
ROLE MAY PERSIST
WHILE
AUTHORITY EXPIRES

Compactly:
Roles may stand. Authority should lease.

This does not require every authorization to use a literal timer.
It means authority should remain conditional on continuing warrant rather than silently becoming tenure.
Crossing
Crossing introduces another distinction:
CONTENT CROSSES
≠
AUTHORITY CROSSES

WARRANT IN
≠
WARRANT OUT

A relation may legitimately enter or form inside a locality without automatically acquiring authority to leave that locality, propagate, scale, redelegate, or create external consequence.
Thus:
INTERNAL FORMATION
≠
EXTERNAL AUTHORIZATION

8. Provision Without Predetermination
Agent Geometry distinguishes structural preparation from mandatory activation.
PROVISIONED
≠
OCCUPIED

AVAILABLE
≠
ACTIVATE

The compact principle is:
PRE-PROVISION
WITHOUT
PRE-DETERMINATION

A system may preserve capacity for future roles, routes, seats, or relations without consuming that capacity immediately.
The deeper reserve taxonomy remains outside this README.
The public proposition is smaller:
Provisioned capacity does not imply required occupancy.

9. Ordinary Cases and Falsification
Agent Geometry should not discover special standing structure everywhere.
It must remain capable of concluding:
ordinary temporary role
ordinary task delegation
ordinary tool access
ordinary worker substitution
ordinary failover
ordinary coordination

without promoting each instance into a load-bearing governance structure.
The central methodological check is:
A ROLE MODEL
THAT CANNOT DISTINGUISH
ORDINARY OCCUPANCY
FROM LOAD-BEARING STANDING
IS NOT USEFUL.

Agent Geometry is therefore intended as an observational architecture, not an ontology generator.
10. Perturbation Harness
A minimal empirical program can begin with a controlled comparison:
FORMATION F0
↓
SMALL REVERSIBLE PERTURBATION
↓
FORMATION F1
↓
COMPARE

Candidate perturbations include:
remove occupant
replace occupant
vacate seat
wipe route history
block dominant route
reduce shared state
expire authority
alter delegation edge
restore locality
re-enter with different occupant

Candidate observations include:
what persists?
what fails?
what reforms?
what migrates?
what becomes expensive?
what remains habitable?
what authority disappears?
what authority incorrectly survives?

The purpose is not to prove an ontology.
It is to test whether the proposed distinctions produce observable differences.

12. Research Questions
Current research questions include:
1. What distinguishes ordinary occupancy from load-bearing standing?
2. What minimum conditions constitute a seat?
3. What must remain invariant for one occupant to substitute for another?
4. Can functional, structural, and authority recovery be independently measured?
5. What authority, if any, should survive delegation, substitution, handoff, re-entry, or remount?
6. When does a route become structurally load-bearing?
7. How should trajectory safety be evaluated when individual actions remain locally permissible?
8. What evidence would justify rejecting Agent Geometry as a useful explanatory layer?
These are research questions, not validated results.
